feat(api): review queue offers each submission awaiting the reviewer (SCRUM-48)

The review queue decided per item. A reviewer who had reviewed anything
on an item was never offered it again. On a task requiring three
annotators with one reviewer, two of the three submissions were never
reviewed. A reviewer who returned work was also locked out of reviewing
the resubmission. And only items in "annotated" were considered, but
returning one annotator's submission sets the whole item to "returned"
while the others still wait.

Hanchen decided on 2026-09-25 that each submission is reviewed on its
own, and that one reviewer may review every submission on an item. The
queue now decides per submission. A submission awaits a reviewer when:
- it still needs a decision: not rejected, returned or escalated since
  it was last submitted, and short of the approvals the task's review
  policy requires;
- and the reviewer is independent of it: not its author, has not
  approved it, and has not reviewed it since it was last submitted.

A resubmission rewrites its annotation in place, so annotations.
updated_at marks the last submission. Approvals are counted over the
whole history, as the review action counts them and as
assert_can_add_approval refuses a repeat. Under dual sign-off, the
second approval therefore still never goes back to the first approver.

An item is offered when at least one of its submissions awaits the
caller, and awaiting_review_annotation_ids names them. A reviewer is
still never offered an item they annotated. Only canonicalized and
disputed items are left out by status.

The rule lives in submission_rules (SubmissionReviewState,
submission_review_states_by_item, awaits_review_by), loaded in two
grouped queries.

Tests: one reviewer reviews all three submissions in turn; dual
sign-off sends the second approval to another reviewer; a returned
submission comes back to its reviewer once resubmitted; returning one
submission leaves the others reviewable; a rejected submission is not
offered again. The first four fail on the previous commit.
test_different_reviewer_is_offered_second_review now sets a dual
sign-off policy, because on a single-pass task one approval completes
the submission and there is no second review to offer.
