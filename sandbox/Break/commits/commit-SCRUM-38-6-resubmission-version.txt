fix(api): a resubmission is a new version, not an overwrite (SCRUM-38)

When a reviewer returned an answer and its author submitted again, the
author's row was rewritten in place: its content replaced, its
submission time moved. The review that returned it still pointed at the
row, now showing an answer the reviewer never saw (R2-2: do not flatten
the history). The same held for an AI model answering an item again.

Every submission now writes a row. The author's first answer is version
1; a later one is create_version'd from the one it follows, for
resubmission or, after a reopen, reanswer_after_reopen. The earlier
version keeps its content, submission time and reviews, and is no longer
current. Approvals belong to a row, so the new version starts with none:
what "approvals since it was last submitted" (#35) meant all along.

assert_may_decide now refuses a submission with no review state (409)
instead of letting it through: a superseded version is history, and no
decision may land on it (#45's nit).

Four tests pinned the in-place rewrite and now pin a new version, each
with its intent kept: a full item still takes its author's resubmission
without a fourth submitter; the draft links to the version it produced;
review starts over on the new version; #46's in-round guard. Three test
helpers faked a resubmission by moving submitted_at; they now write a
version, and their nine callers review the version they return.

Story F3, SCRUM-38.
